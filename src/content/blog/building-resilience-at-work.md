## Rethinking Resilience

      

Resilience is one of the most talked-about concepts in the modern workplace, yet it is also one of the most misunderstood. Too often, resilience is equated with toughness — the ability to grit your teeth and push through adversity. This framing is not only inaccurate; it can be actively harmful, placing the burden of systemic problems on the shoulders of individuals.

      

True resilience is something quite different. It is the capacity to adapt, recover and grow in the face of challenges. It is not about never struggling; it is about having the skills and support systems to navigate difficulty effectively. And crucially, it is a skill that can be developed — not a fixed personality trait.

      

## The Business Case for Resilience

      

Organisations that invest in building resilience see tangible benefits:

      

        - Lower rates of absenteeism and burnout.
        - Higher levels of engagement and discretionary effort.
        - Greater adaptability during periods of change and uncertainty.
        - Improved team cohesion and psychological safety.
      

      

In a world where change is constant and pressures are mounting, resilience is not a luxury — it is a core organisational capability.

      

## Practical Strategies for Individuals

      

Building personal resilience starts with awareness. Here are evidence-based strategies that we incorporate into our training programmes:

      

        - **Reframe your thinking:** Cognitive reframing — the practice of challenging unhelpful thought patterns and finding alternative perspectives — is one of the most powerful resilience tools available. When faced with a setback, ask yourself: "What can I learn from this?" and "What is within my control?"
        - **Invest in recovery:** Resilience is not about relentless effort. It requires deliberate recovery. Build regular breaks, exercise, sleep and activities that recharge you into your routine. High performance depends on sustainable rhythms, not constant sprinting.
        - **Strengthen your connections:** Social support is one of the strongest predictors of resilience. Cultivate relationships with colleagues you trust, seek out mentors and do not hesitate to ask for help when you need it.
        - **Develop a growth mindset:** People who view challenges as opportunities to learn — rather than threats to their competence — bounce back more quickly and perform better under pressure.
      

      

## Building Resilient Teams and Organisations

      

While individual resilience matters, organisations have a responsibility to create the conditions in which resilience can flourish. This means:

      

        - Leaders modelling healthy behaviours — taking breaks, admitting mistakes and asking for feedback.
        - Creating psychological safety so that people feel able to speak up, raise concerns and take risks without fear of blame.
        - Providing access to development opportunities, coaching and wellbeing resources.
        - Reviewing workloads and processes to ensure that demands are reasonable and sustainable.
      

      

Resilience is not about expecting people to cope with whatever is thrown at them. It is about equipping them with the skills, mindset and support they need to thrive — and creating a workplace that makes that possible.
