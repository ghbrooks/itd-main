## The ROI Question

      

Every investment in training should ultimately deliver a measurable return. For sales training, the connection between learning and revenue makes this easier to quantify than many other forms of development — yet surprisingly few organisations track it rigorously.

      

At ITD, we believe that measuring the impact of sales training is not something you do after the programme is delivered. It is something you plan for from the very start. Here is how to approach it.

      

## Establishing a Baseline

      

Before any training takes place, you need a clear picture of current performance. Key metrics to capture include:

      

        - **Conversion rates:** What percentage of leads convert to opportunities, and opportunities to closed deals?
        - **Average deal size:** What is the typical value of a closed sale?
        - **Sales cycle length:** How long does it take from first contact to signed contract?
        - **Customer retention rates:** How many existing customers renew or expand their contracts?
        - **Activity levels:** How many calls, meetings and proposals is each salesperson generating?
      

      

These baseline numbers give you a reference point against which to measure improvement. Without them, any post-training claims of success are anecdotal at best.

      

## The Four Levels of Evaluation

      

The Kirkpatrick model remains the most widely used framework for evaluating training effectiveness. Applied to sales training, it works like this:

      

        - **Level 1 — Reaction:** Did participants find the training valuable and relevant? Capture this through post-course feedback forms.
        - **Level 2 — Learning:** Did participants acquire new knowledge and skills? Assess this through role plays, assessments or observed practice during the programme.
        - **Level 3 — Behaviour:** Are participants applying what they learned back in the field? This requires follow-up observation, manager feedback and coaching conversations in the weeks after training.
        - **Level 4 — Results:** Has the training delivered measurable business outcomes? Compare your post-training metrics against the baseline you established earlier.
      

      

Most organisations do a reasonable job at Levels 1 and 2 but fall short at Levels 3 and 4. This is where the real value lies.

      

## Calculating the Financial Return

      

To calculate ROI in financial terms, use a straightforward formula: **(Gain from training − Cost of training) / Cost of training x 100**. The gain might be measured as incremental revenue, improved margins or reduced cost of sale. Be conservative in your assumptions and transparent about your methodology — a credible, modest ROI figure is far more persuasive than an inflated one.

      

## Making It Sustainable

      

The biggest mistake organisations make with sales training is treating it as a one-off event. Skills fade without reinforcement. Build in regular coaching sessions, refresher workshops and peer learning groups to sustain momentum. When you do, the ROI compounds over time.
